Electric vehicle AC charging · up to 22 kW

The AC network the car actually sees

A home charging station is a controlled connection to the public low-voltage grid — not a DC charger. What the vehicle can draw depends on voltage, phase count, earthing, national rules, the inlet on the car, and the onboard charger. This overview maps those facts worldwide.

Earthing concepts (IEC 60364)

The first letter is the source. The second is how the installation earths. S/C describe whether PE and N are separate or combined. The EV circuit itself must never contain a PEN conductor.

Open-PEN on TN-C-S / PME: if the combined PEN conductor breaks, the car body (via PE in the charge cable) can rise toward line voltage relative to true earth. UK/IE require extra measures for outdoor charging (open-PEN disconnect at 70 V, a TT island, or electrical separation). IEC 60364-7-722: no PEN on the EV circuit.

Special cases

These break the “230/400 V Type 2 everywhere” mental model.

Norway — 230 V IT (~70 % of LV)

Three lines at 230 V to each other, often no neutral. Default home charging: single-phase 3.7–7.4 kW. Three-phase IT (~11 kW at ~28 A) only if the onboard charger is specified for 230 V 3φ (some Teslas; not universal). Newer areas are TN 400 V. Blue IEC 60309 3φ 230 V is not red 400 V.

UK & Ireland — PME open-PEN + single-phase homes

Most houses are 1φ 60–100 A, Type 2, 7.4 kW. 22 kW is not a typical dwelling option. Outdoor charging on PME needs open-PEN protection (disconnect L, N and PE if PE–earth > 70 V), a suitable TT island, or electrical separation (BS 7671 722.411.4.1).

Belgium — 3×230 V delta pockets (no N)

Some LV islands have no distributed neutral; loads sit across two phases. EVSE that need a neutral–PE reference can refuse to start. Survey before specifying; an isolating transformer to local TN-S is the robust fix.

NA — 277 V is 480Y commercial, not a US region

277 V = L–N of a 480 V wye (\(480/\sqrt{3}\)), same idea as 230 V from 400 V in Europe. It appears nationwide in large commercial and industrial buildings (lighting, some workplace AC EVSE). It is not a house voltage. Homes are 120/240 V split-phase. 80 A × 277 V ≈ 22 kW is the North American AC ceiling (NACS/J1772), not 3φ 400 V. Many Level 2 boxes are 208–240 V only — do not land them on 277 V. DCFC uses 480 V three-phase on the same service.

NA — 208Y/120 apartments

Multi-dwelling and small commercial are often 208 V L–L, 120 V L–N. Level 2 at 208 V is ~13 % below a 240 V nameplate (32 A → 6.7 kW). The EVSE must be listed 208–240 V.

NA — high-leg 240 V delta and 480 V delta

Older commercial 120/240 V 3φ delta: the orange high leg is 208 V to N. Never put 120 V loads or L–N EVSE on it. Measure L–N: 120/120/208 means high-leg, not 208Y (120/120/120). 480 V delta (ungrounded or corner-grounded) has no 277 V because there is no wye neutral.

NA — Canada 347 V, Mexico 127/220 V, couplers

Canadian large commercial is often 600Y/347 V (347 V lighting = 277 V analogue). Mexico dwellings are often 127/220 V, not 120/240 V. Vehicle AC is J1772 or NACS, single-phase, almost always tethered. SAE J3068 (Type 2 3φ) is depot/fleet, not houses. NEC 625: breaker ≥ 125 % of charger current. UL 2231 CCID is not an IEC Type B RCD.

Taiwan — 110/220 V 60 Hz; 40 A is real (mostly the breaker)

Homes are 1φ 3-wire 110/220 V at 60 Hz (split-phase, not 120/240). Wallboxes sit on a dedicated 220 V circuit. Default: 32 A @ 220 V ≈ 7.0 kW, J1772 / CNS 15511 (Tesla TW also Type 2). That 32 A EVSE is paired with a 40 A NFB (125 % continuous) — Tesla’s TW home page quotes a 40 A breaker for Model 3/Y. 40 A charger output also exists (Evalue X40A: 40 A / 8.8 kW @ 220 V on a 50 A RCCB). 48–80 A SKUs are sold; they need the service capacity. Buildings may be 3φ 3-wire 220 V or 3φ 4-wire 220/380 V.

Japan — 100/200 V, 50/60 Hz, Type 1, TT

Home charging is 200 V single-phase Type 1, typically 3–6 kW. 100 V is trickle. East 50 Hz / west 60 Hz. Many Type A sockets are ungrounded — do not use them as a daily EV supply.

China — GB/T AC is not Type 2

The 7-pin AC coupler looks like Type 2 but the gender is reversed and signalling is CC/CP, not PP/CP. Home piles are typically 7 kW 1φ 32 A @ 220 V. Voltage band GB/T 12325: +7 / −10 %.

Saudi Arabia — IEC voltages at 60 Hz

230/400 V, Type G household, Type 2 vehicle, TN-S — but 60 Hz. Villas often have three-phase service, so 11/22 kW is realistic.

When is 22 kW actually available at home?

Only if the dwelling has 3φ 400 V, the main fuse / contracted power can spare 3×32 A, the car’s onboard charger is 22 kW (minority), and the DSO allows it. Otherwise specify 11 kW (3×16 A) or 7.4 kW (1×32 A).
